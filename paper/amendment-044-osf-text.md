# Amendment 44: Joseph Smith's revelatory and non-revelatory writing compared (BOM-32)

*Filed 2026-11-10. Post hoc. Context: `docs/results-first-read.md` section 8.*

---

**Amendment 44, filed 2026-11-10, post hoc: decided after unblinding, on the owner's question.**
In the rerun without Book of Mormon quotations (Amendment 43), every method ranks Joseph Smith's
pre-1830 sample closest to the text. That sample is entirely revelation (1828-29), written in a
scriptural voice like the Book of Mormon's, while every other candidate sample is in another
register, so the ranking may reflect register rather than personal style. No non-revelatory
writing of his survives from before 1830 beyond one 356-word scribal copy. His non-revelatory
writing of 1831-33 (dictated letters and the handwritten letters and 1832 history, together about
11,300 words, over the 10,000-word floor) is pooled into one candidate unit, scored on the sealed
set by the classical methods and by candidate models at 125M, 297M and 590M exactly as the other
units (same base models, settings and seeds rule), in the Amendment 43 setting. The report sets
the revelatory sample, the pooled non-revelatory sample and the separate letters side by side. As
for every post-1830 sample, closeness is uninformative about authorship (he had read the book) and
distance is informative. Reported as a stated limitation of the study, not as an attribution.
