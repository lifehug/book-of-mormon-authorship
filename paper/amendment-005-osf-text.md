# Amendment 5: expanded candidate corpora as a registered secondary analysis

*Paste-ready text for https://osf.io/6ugxc/. Reasoning in `docs/audit.md` and BOM-48, BOM-49.*

---

**Amendment 5, [DATE], before any calibration or Book of Mormon scoring.** The Book of Mormon
text remains sealed under 4.2 until BOM-32, no method has been calibrated, and no candidate has
been scored.

**Nothing in this amendment replaces anything.** The candidate corpora fixed in section 3, and
every decision rule in section 6, are unchanged and remain the confirmatory analysis. H1a and
H1b are decided on them and on nothing else. Everything registered below is a *secondary*
analysis, reported in its own section, and may not be substituted for the primary result in any
case — including, and especially, the case where the primary result is inconclusive.

**What prompted this.** Section 10 concedes that Joseph Smith's undisputed pre-1830 writing is
small and that he will likely fall under the sample floor for most methods. A power analysis
(BOM-43) will establish how far under, before any analysis runs. The obvious remedy is his much
larger later corpus, and the obvious objection is that he preached and quoted the Book of Mormon
for fourteen years, so resemblance between his later writing and the text is equally consistent
with his having written it and with his having absorbed it. The remedy is registered here in the
only form in which it carries information.

**Change 1: exposure classification, fixed in advance.** Each candidate is assigned an exposure
tier from the documented historical record, and the assignment is published before any scoring:

- *No later writing.* Solomon Spalding, died 1816. No expansion is possible and none is
  attempted.
- *Exposed.* Joseph Smith, Oliver Cowdery, Sidney Rigdon, Parley P. Pratt, W. W. Phelps — all
  of whom read, preached from, or published on the Book of Mormon for years after 1830.
- *Unexposed.* Ethan Smith, Gilbert Hunt, and any author drawn from the corpus at large, who
  had no sustained involvement with the text.

**Change 2: the one-directional rule for exposed candidates.** Exposure biases an expanded
corpus *toward* resemblance. Accordingly, for a candidate in the exposed tier, a *non-match* on
the expanded corpus is reported as evidence and a *match* is reported as uninformative. This is
fixed here precisely so it cannot be chosen after the direction of the result is known. For
unexposed candidates the expanded corpus is used in both directions, subject to Change 4.

**Change 3: provenance tiers.** Later material is not uniformly the candidate's language. Much
of the post-1830 record is reported speech taken down by hearers, or was composed and edited by
others. Each sample is tiered as *holograph*, *dictated to a scribe*, *reported speech*, or
*composed or edited by others*. Only holograph and dictated samples enter attribution; the lower
two tiers are catalogued, reported, and excluded. Each candidate's word count is published per
tier.

**Change 4: the expansion must earn its use, per candidate.** An expanded corpus is used for a
candidate only if that candidate's authorial signal is shown to be stable across the same time
gap. The test (BOM-48) builds a profile from a candidate's later writing and attributes their
earlier writing, and uses known authors only, so it breaks no seal and runs before any Book of
Mormon work. A candidate whose time-split accuracy falls below the section 7 floor does not get
an expanded corpus, and that fact is published. Time-split accuracy is reported for every
candidate whether it supports the extension or not.

**Change 5: extension to author-sweep leads.** A lead that becomes a candidate under section 3's
existing rule may also receive an expanded corpus under Changes 1 to 4. Two limits are fixed
here: at most **ten** leads receive an expansion, taken in rank order from those passing section
3's plausibility requirement; and the expansion changes nothing about section 3's requirement
that a promoted lead is tested on the confirmation half only, which the sweep never sees.

**Change 6: the number of tests is published.** The paper reports the total count of
candidate-by-method combinations run, primary and secondary separately, so that the
multiple-comparison burden of the secondary analysis is visible rather than implicit. A study
that tested eight candidates and one that tested eighty do not warrant the same reading of the
same threshold.

**Direction of this change.** It adds analyses and adds constraints on them; it removes nothing
and relaxes no threshold. The honest expectation, stated here in advance, is that it will help
least where it is needed most: Joseph Smith is both the candidate shortest of text and the
candidate most exposed, so his expansion is confined to the one-directional rule, while the
candidates whose expansions are cleanest are the published authors who likely already clear the
sample floor. This amendment is not expected to rescue the attribution question for Joseph
Smith, and it is registered now so that it cannot later be presented as though it had.
