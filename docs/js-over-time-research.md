# Joseph Smith's prose over time: sources and attribution (research for BOM-73)

Research report, 2026-11-10, by a research agent (Claude Fable 5.1) reading public sources; every
claim carries its URL. Word counts marked "est." are the agent's estimates, "repo" are this
project's measured counts. Nothing here is a result of the study; it is the groundwork for the
tabled issue BOM-73 (does Joseph Smith's prose drift toward the Book of Mormon's style after
1830, beside exposed and unexposed writers of the same years?).

Why we did not already hold these documents: BOM-13 chose candidate samples near 1830 (the study
asks who could have written the book), and deliberately left out later papers commonly composed
by clerks or ghost-writers (`docs/candidates.md`). The fetching tools exist (`ingest.candidates`,
`ingest.jsp`); the hard part is attribution, not access.

### 1.5 Q2: who classifies by hand and authorship

- Dean C. Jessee, *Personal Writings of Joseph Smith* (1984; rev. 2002, Deseret Book/BYU Press): holographs set in large type; two sections (diaries/autobiography; letters). [BYU Studies review](https://byustudies.byu.edu/article/the-personal-writings-of-joseph-smith). Modern edition, copyrighted.
- Jessee, "The Writing of Joseph Smith's History", BYU Studies 11:4 (1971) 439-473, [ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/byusq/vol11/iss4/8); "Priceless Words and Fallible Memories", BYU Studies 31:2 (1991), [ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/byusq/vol31/iss2/4); "Joseph Smith Jr.—In His Own Words", Ensign 1984 (holograph count).
- JSP: every document page has a Source Note with handwriting per scribe, and the transcript marks his hand in bold; plus the handwriting list above. There is no published JSP table of word counts by hand.
- Yes, a published holograph list exists: the JSP page above (35 letters/documents plus the journal entries); Jessee 1984 counted 29 + two diary portions.

### 1.7 Q4: prior work

| Study | What it did with Smith's own writing | Drift over time? |
|---|---|---|
| Larson, Rencher, Layton 1980 (BYU Studies 20:3) | JS sample "did not match any of the styles within the Book of Mormon" | no |

### 1.8 Q5: my per-period estimate of reliably-his non-revelatory prose

| Period | Holograph | Dictated, clearly his | Verdict at a 2,000-word floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1829-30 | 0 | <1,500 | **too thin** (and what exists is scribal copies) |
| 1831-33 | ~8,000 | ~15,000-20,000 | usable; best period |
| 1834-36 | ~3,000 | ~30,000 journal (dictated but mixed with minutes), ~5,000 letters | usable if the 1835-36 journal is admitted and screened entry by entry; holograph alone marginal |
| 1837-39 | ~3,500 | ~12,000-15,000 (Liberty Jail letters dominate; strongly scriptural register) | usable; 1837 alone is empty |
| 1840-44 | ~1,500 prose | ~8,000-15,000 secure; much more if clerk-penned letters admitted | marginal on secure text; the key period is the hardest to attribute |

Holograph-only time series: only 1831-33 clears 2,000 words comfortably; 1834-36 and 1837-39 sit at 3,000-3,500; 1840-44 fails. A dictated-plus-holograph series clears the floor in every period after 1830, at the cost of scribe effects that Jockers 2013 is the only attempt to measure.

Hardest to obtain, in order: (1) 2,000+ words per period of *securely* Smith-composed 1840-44 prose — the secure set is ~1,500 holograph plus ~10,000 dictated, and the rest depends on clerk attribution that only Jockers 2013 (paywalled) has tested; (2) Cowdery and Phelps in 1837-39 and Cowdery in 1840-44 (both under 2,000 words digitized); (3) regionally matched unexposed controls — Ohio/Missouri religious periodicals of 1830-44 are mostly behind ProQuest, and Chronicling America's 1830s is DC/Virginia-heavy; (4) signature-level attribution inside the Mormon periodicals, where editorship changed hands and the repo's rule 11-17 signed-item filter is the right instrument.

Two things I could not verify: the full-text results of Jockers 2013 (abstract only; the 2009 "at least 20 of 109" figure is preliminary), and whether JSP's "Rights and Use" policy differs from the Church's general terms (page not retrievable).
