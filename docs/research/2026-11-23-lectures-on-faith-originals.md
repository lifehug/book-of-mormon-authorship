## Phipps 1977, "The Lectures on Faith: An Authorship Study" (MA thesis, English, BYU, April 1977)

- Candidates: Smith, Rigdon, Pratt, Cowdery, Phelps, proposed by Peter Crawley (p. 10). He used
  Smith's holograph writing to avoid his scribes (p. 10).
- Samples (Table 7, p. 18; Tables 2-6, pp. 13-17), all with quotations removed by hand (p. 17,
  pp. 23-24):
  - Lectures 12,006 words: L1 1,155; L2 2,852; L3 1,187; L4 1,906; L5 744; L6 1,233; L7 2,929.
    The catechisms (question-and-answer) at the lecture ends were deleted (p. 24).
  - Smith 25,476 words:
    - Messenger and Advocate articles, 8,243: "JS's Youth", Dec 1834, 1:3 p. 40; "Gathering",
      Sep, Nov and Dec 1835; "Abolitionists", Apr 1836.
    - Journal, 27 Nov 1832 to 5 Dec 1834, 5,594.
    - Letters, 11,639, from 22 Feb 1831 (Martin Harris) to 27 Jun 1844 (Emma; lawyer Browning).
      The per-letter counts are approximate (p. 14 n. a).
  - Rigdon 12,237: "Gospel" Nos. 1-8 (Star Sep 1834; M&A Nov 1834 - Jun 1835) and "Faith of the
    Church" No. 8 (M&A Nov 1834), all unsigned in the papers (Table 3, p. 14).
  - Pratt 12,289: *Voice of Warning* (1837) pp. iii-x and 11-61, 9,231; *A Short Account of a
    Shameful Outrage* (1835?) secs. 1-3, 3,058 (Table 4, p. 15).
  - Cowdery 13,033: M&A letters Nos. 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 (Oct 1834 - Jul 1835) (Table 5, p. 16).
  - Phelps 12,652: "To Man" (Star, Jun 1832); M&A letters Nos. 4, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12; "To the
    Saints" (Jun 1835); 2nd series No. 1 (Jul 1836) (Table 6, p. 17).
  - Grand total 87,693 (p. 18).
- Features:
  - 738 words and phrases, listed in Table 29 (appendix, p. 72 on). Phrases were typed as one word
    (e.g. "inasmuchas", p. 25). Many words carry part-of-speech codes 1-9, e.g. "that2", "to7"
    (Table 9, p. 22).
  - "High frequency" means at least 1 per 1,000 words over all candidates combined: 84 words, of
    which 34 were dropped as contextual (personal pronouns, most verbs, words like "man", "world").
    That leaves "the fifty" (pp. 33-34), printed in Table 13 (pp. 35-39). I can read 49 rows in
    the scan, and 25 of them carry a code.
  - Low frequency: 654 words (p. 41).
- Method (pp. 26-32): Mosteller and Wallace's simplified approach.
  - Six 2,000-word blocks per candidate. Smith's 12 blocks were averaged in adjacent pairs to make
    six (p. 27 n. 2).
  - For each word and each pair of candidates: take the median of the 12 block rates, count each
    man's blocks above and below it (a 2x2 table), and check which side the lectures' mean falls
    on. The odds are then multiplied across words.
  - Rates are rounded down to whole numbers per 1,000 (p. 32). A word is skipped for a pair when
    the pair's median is within 1 per 1,000 of the lectures' mean (p. 34). Odds of zero are not
    used (p. 32).
  - Thirteen tests in all (Table 26, p. 63). Phipps says some were planned in advance and others
    "grew out of" earlier results (p. 26).
- Headline numbers:
  - Test 1, combined lectures, 50 words (Table 13, p. 39): Rigdon over Smith 291:1, over Pratt
    174,883:1, over Cowdery 1,694:1, over Phelps 37,154:1.
  - Test 4, strongest discriminators (Table 16, p. 44): Rigdon over Smith 206:1, over Pratt
    3,158,452:1, over Cowdery 16,991:1, over Phelps 85,357:1. He warns that odds in the millions
    are unreliable (p. 44 n. a).
  - Test 8, Smith against Rigdon per lecture, 18 words (Table 20, p. 50): L1 Rigdon 2,623,830:1;
    L2 Smith 14:1; L3 Rigdon 10:1; L4 Smith 13:1; L5 Smith 556,700:1; L6 Smith 31:1; L7 Rigdon
    7,413,299:1.
  - Per-lecture tests 6-11 (Table 26, p. 63): L2 goes to Smith in tests 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11, and to
    Rigdon only in test 9 (low-frequency words).
  - Test 9 (low-frequency words) gives all but L5 to Rigdon "by rather comfortable margins"
    (p. 64).
  - Final conclusion (Table 28, p. 67): L1 Rigdon; L2 Rigdon, likely editing by Smith; L3 and L4
    Rigdon, possible editing by Smith; L5 Smith; L6 Rigdon, likely editing by Smith; L7 Rigdon.
  - Paragraph-level editing claims: Tables 25 and 27 (pp. 58, 65).
- No known-answer test: none of the 13 tests attributes writing of known authorship (pp. 26-59).
  Test 10 (pp. 52-54) only compares Smith's three classes of writing against Rigdon on the
  lectures.
- An internal inconsistency in Phipps. The text says Smith "dominates all candidates except" Rigdon
  (p. 40). But Table 13's combined odds (p. 39) favour Pratt over Smith (1:1.83) and Cowdery over
  Smith (1:1.30). Test 4 (p. 44) then favours Smith over Pratt (2.6:1) and Cowdery over Smith
  (1:5.5).

## Larsen, Rencher and Layton 1980, "Who Wrote the Book of Mormon? An Analysis of Wordprints", BYU Studies 20, no. 3: 225-251

- Book of Mormon claims:
  - Multiple authors. Twenty-four "authors" (speakers) with at least about 1,000 words (Appendix B,
    p. 246), in 251 blocks of about 1,000 words (p. 232).
  - MANOVA on the 10 commonest words: odds of a single author "less than 1 in 100 billion"
    (p. 232). The same with 38 words (p. 233). With the non-Book of Mormon authors added (33
    authors, 341 blocks), under 1 in 10 billion (p. 234).
  - Discriminant analysis: 93.3% of 2,000-word blocks correct for 21 authors with 18 words
    (p. 238); 70-80% when blocks are held out (p. 238). Four major authors plus Smith: 96.7%
    (p. 242). Across analyses, held-out accuracy ran 50-81% (p. 245).
  - Conclusion: authorship "conclusively" multiple (pp. 234, 245).
- Claims about Smith, Rigdon, Spalding and Cowdery:
  - "None of the Book of Mormon selections resembled the writing of any of the suggested
    nineteenth century authors" (p. 234). Conclusion 3 (pp. 244-245) says the evidence "should
    discredit" the theories that Smith, Spalding or others wrote it.
  - In the plot of the first two discriminant functions the two groups separate cleanly (Fig. 1,
    p. 239; p. 245).
  - Translation "direct and literal" (p. 244).
- Smith and Rigdon samples: taken from Phipps's own disk (p. 230 n. 15).
  - Smith: Messenger and Advocate articles, journal and letters, "his own words".
  - Rigdon: from the Star and the Messenger and Advocate.
  - Pratt: *Voice of Warning* and *Shameful Outrage*. Cowdery: six M&A letters. Phelps: Star and
    M&A.
  - Also used: D&C 101 and 104; Spalding (five random selections from *Manuscript Found*); the
    unsigned "Paracletes" (*Times and Seasons*), whose results were "consistently inconsistent"
    (p. 230 n. 16).
  - Ninety non-Book of Mormon blocks (p. 233), from 9 non-Book of Mormon authors (p. 234).
- Features (p. 231):
  - Three wordprint definitions: letter frequencies, 38 common words, 42 uncommon words, all listed
    in Appendix C (p. 247).
  - The list was built from Book of Mormon frequencies, so it includes "unto", "came", "pass",
    "behold", "yea".
  - Arcsine transform (p. 232 n. 18).
- Lectures on Faith (Appendix E, p. 249):
  - A cluster analysis of the 9 non-Book of Mormon authors pairs the Lectures with Rigdon.
  - Discriminant analysis: each lecture is one block. The computation set is "7 non-Book of
    Mormon authors with 36 blocks of 2000 words". Eight words are used (not named) and four
    functions are kept.
  - 88.9% of computation-set blocks were classified correctly. This is resubstitution, since the
    same blocks built the functions.
  - Per-lecture classification, first choice (probability), second choice:
    - L1 Rigdon (1.0)
    - L2 Smith (.524), Rigdon (.339)
    - L3 Rigdon (1.0)
    - L4 Rigdon (.988), Smith (.005)
    - L5 Phelps (.461), Pratt (.367)
    - L6 Rigdon (1.00)
    - L7 Rigdon (.995), Smith (.005)
  - L5 is "rather distant" from the Rigdon group and has "only 772 words" (Phipps: 744).
  - They describe Phipps correctly: L1 and L7 Rigdon, L5 Smith, L2, L3, L4 and L6 collaborative.
- An internal inconsistency in Larsen. Item 4 on p. 234 says Smith's writing is "very different"
  from the Lectures. The 34-author cluster on p. 236 says Smith's blocks "combined with" the
  Lectures, then with Cowdery's. Appendix E (p. 249) pairs the Lectures with Rigdon. All three
  cite Appendix E.
- "Larsen and Rencher 1982" (the issue and the blog) is the reprint in Reynolds, ed., *Book of
  Mormon Authorship* (1982). Reynolds also cites a 1996 FARMS printing, pp. 183-84 (Reynolds 2005
  p. 10 n. 28), and calls it "revised". We hold only the 1980 original. I have not seen whether the
  revision changed Appendix E.

## Reynolds 2005, "The Case for Sidney Rigdon as Author of the Lectures on Faith", Journal of Mormon History, Fall 2005, pp. 1-41

- Bibliographic details:
  - The printed contents page reads "Volume 32, No. 3, Fall 2005". The repository cover page reads
    Vol. 31, Iss. 2. studies.json has 31, no. 3, the usual citation.
  - A note on p. 42 (PDF p. 53) says this printing replaces the Summer 2005 one (vol. 31, pp. 70-88),
    which had "an incorrect formula and a missing table".
- Earlier studies, as he summarises them:
  - Partridge 1976, a qualitative style study for the Church Historical Department: Smith "neither
    wrote nor dictated the major portion" (pp. 8-9).
  - Phipps (p. 9): Rigdon except L5 to Smith. Correct against the thesis.
  - Larsen and Rencher (p. 10): Rigdon for all but possibly 2 and 5; L5 Phelps or Pratt; L2 weakly
    Smith, Rigdon close. Correct.
  - "Three independent authorship studies ... all conclude" Rigdon (p. 8).
- His own test (pp. 27-29; Appendix 1 pp. 35-37; Appendix 2 pp. 37-41):
  - Rigdon's three serialised essays: "Faith of the Church of Christ in These Last Days" (15
    parts), "Millenium" (14 parts) and "Gospel" (8 parts), Star and M&A, Dec 1833 - Aug 1835.
    Reynolds: "We know that Rigdon wrote the essays" (p. 27).
  - A hired freelance writer, told nothing of the purpose, read only Rigdon's essays and listed 38
    distinctive phrases in five groups. They were counted with WordCruncher in the essays, the
    Lectures, and Smith's letters 1829-34 and diaries 1832-36 (the Infobase / GospeLink 1998
    texts, p. 28 n. 84).
  - Word counts: Smith 78,587, Rigdon 77,013, Lectures 23,750 (p. 28).
  - Results: 163 occurrences in the essays, 98 in the Lectures (all 38 occur at least once), 0 in
    Smith. Rates 4.1 vs 2.1 per 1,000 (p. 28). I checked the arithmetic: 98/23.75 = 4.13 and
    163/77.013 = 2.12.
  - Conclusion: the "smoking gun". It "does not seem reasonable" to maintain any other author
    (p. 35).
- What I see as its weaknesses:
  - The phrases were chosen from Rigdon's text only, so absence from Smith is partly built in.
  - No other doctrinal writer of the period (Cowdery, Phelps, Pratt) was counted.
  - The Rigdon essays are unsigned, and he assumes their authorship.
  - "Independent" is too strong for Larsen, who used Phipps's texts.
  - He says Larsen and Rencher "had a stronger set of comparison texts" than Phipps (p. 10). But
    Larsen's candidate texts are Phipps's disk (Larsen p. 230 n. 15).

Method:

- Phipps: specified well enough to rerun (pp. 27-34), and the 50 words are printed (Table 13).
  Missing: his hand-removed text, his per-block counts ("not reproduced in this thesis", p. 27
  n. 1), and his hand part-of-speech coding for 25 of the words. So an exact rerun is impossible
  and an approximate one is cheap.
- Larsen: the 38 words are printed (p. 247), but the 8 stepwise-chosen words for the Lectures and
  the exact composition of "7 authors, 36 blocks" are not given. Approximate rerun only.
- Reynolds: the 38 phrases and their locations are printed (pp. 37-41), and the texts are named.
  This is the most exactly reproducible of the three, apart from the GospeLink Smith edition (we
  have JSP transcripts of the same letters and diaries).

Reproduction plan (all local CPU, no model judging, no GPU; nothing run, nothing written into the
repo):

1. Rebuild Phipps's five samples from the held texts per Tables 2-6. Cut quotations with the
   existing King James and scripture n-gram drops (`lab.twovoices.drop_kjv`, the v2 `quote_ngram`
   rule) in place of his hand removal. Substitute same-year held letters for the four missing Smith
   letters and later *Voice of Warning* pages for *Shameful Outrage*, and flag both. Cut the
   lectures at their headings and drop the catechism, as v2 does.
2. Larsen's way: 2,000-word blocks for each man; the 38 Appendix C words as arcsine rates; stepwise
   linear discriminant analysis picking 8 words; 4 functions; each whole lecture classified with
   posterior probabilities. Compare to the p. 249 table. Also report leave-one-block-out accuracy,
   which they did not. A few dozen lines (scikit-learn or numpy), seconds.
3. Phipps's way:
   - Tests 1 and 4 on the 24 uncoded words of the 50 (the, of, and, in, a, it, with, from, or,
     upon, at, on, would, if, things, an, its, should, great, into, can, unto, must, let).
   - Optionally all 50 with the codes approximated by a deterministic part-of-speech tagger, run
     locally (a tagger, not a model reading for judgement; say which).
   - Six blocks each (Smith's 12 averaged in pairs), median-split 2x2 odds, his rounding and skip
     rules, odds multiplied.
   - Compare to 291:1 (Rigdon over Smith, combined) and to Table 20 per lecture.
   - Then the step he skipped: the same odds on held-back blocks of each man.
4. Reynolds's way: count the 38 phrases, as regular expressions written from Appendix 2, in
   Rigdon's three series, Smith's 1829-36 letters and journal, and the Lectures. Compare to
   163 / 98 / 0. Then count the same 38 in Cowdery's, Phelps's and Pratt's 1832-37 doctrinal prose
   (held), the control his design lacked. If the decoys use them at Rigdon-like rates, the phrases
   mark the period's sermon style, not Rigdon.
5. Then the planned resampled interval on each lecture's winner (unchanged).
