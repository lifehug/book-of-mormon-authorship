# What the CES Letter names as sources (for paper section 6)

Checked 2026-11-10 against the Letter's Book of Mormon section,
https://read.cesletter.org/bom/ (Jeremy Runnells; sub-headings in order: 1769 KJV Errors, 17th
Century Italics, Mistranslations, DNA, Anachronisms, Archaeology, Geography, Hill Cumorah, View
of the Hebrews, The Late War, The First Book of Napoleon, Early Godhead).

| What the Letter names | Its argument | What this study tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *View of the Hebrews*, Ethan Smith (1823; 2nd ed. 1825) | B. H. Roberts' parallels, shown chart by chart | wording, rare phrases, paraphrase, story structure, Ethan Smith as a candidate author, access (claim c02) |
| *The Late War between the United States and Great Britain*, Gilbert J. Hunt (the Letter gives 1819; first edition 1816) | shared phrasing, a school text | the same, Hunt as a candidate author (claim c01) |
| *The First Book of Napoleon* (1809) | shared phrasing | wording, rare phrases, paraphrase, story structure (named-source claim) |
| King James Bible: 1769-edition errors, translators' italics (2 Ne 19:1, 3 Ne 24:10), Isaiah, the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 6:25-27) against the later Joseph Smith Translation | the text follows a printed KJV | not tested: the study masks every KJV-aligned span (6.8% of the text) and does not ask which edition it follows |
| Captain Kidd stories, an 1808 map of Africa ("Camora", "Moroni"), Pomeroy Tucker, Vernal Holley's place-name table | names and reading environment | not tested (place names and biography are outside the study's methods) |

Not named by the Letter: Solomon Spalding's *Manuscript Story* (tested here because other critics
name it, claim register), Josiah Priest. Section 6 of the paper covers the three books the Letter
names; Spalding gets the same treatment beside them, labelled as not from the Letter.
